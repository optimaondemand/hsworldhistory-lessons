⚠️ APPENDIX A: STANDARDS GAP (W.2.6, ICONOCLASM HALF)

SS.912.W.2.6 (Iconoclast Controversy half only) has no natural home in the assigned weekly primary sources: Week 2's sources are Justinian-era, roughly two centuries too early for the 8th–9th century Iconoclast Controversy. Appendix A supplies a primary source exercise on the controversy (excerpt from the Acts of the Second Council of Nicaea, 787), cross-referenced as supplementary reading for the Week 2 Byzantine unit or as background before Week 9's treatment of the 1054 Schism. The Schism half of W.2.6 is assessed at Week 9; the code as a whole is therefore satisfied, even though no single item tags both halves.

⚠️ FOUR REMAINING GAPS, CLOSED FROM EIGHT AT SESSION S3: NONE BLOCKING

Session S3 (2026-08-03) tagged all 16 VR activities with the benchmarks their own content genuinely exercises (not copied wholesale from their week's lessons), added a new inference question to Week 2's reading quiz, and tagged three ELA.EE codes against the Primary Source Exercise and discussion rubrics' own criteria. This closed five of the original eight gaps (W.2.1, W.2.5, W.2.14, W.2.19, G.4.9) and part of a sixth (the Japan cluster, W.2.19 of four).

Four GAP rows remain, deliberately not tagged around: W.2.20, W.2.21, and W.2.22 (the Week 10 VR activity is genuinely only about physiography and the failed invasion of Japan, not feudalism, culture, or Sino-Korean relations), and ELA.EE.5.1 (no rubric anywhere in the course scores document format or citation rules as its own criterion). Each needs a new question, exercise, or rubric criterion to close honestly, not a tag on existing content.

Full reasoning behind every gap, closed and open, is in standards-index.md (course root).
