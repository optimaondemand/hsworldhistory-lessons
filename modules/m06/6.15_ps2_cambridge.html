The 1231 Cambridge Royal Charter
Primary Source Reading · read before your VR session
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

This charter, issued by King Henry III in 1231, protected Cambridge's community of masters and scholars, including from tension with local townspeople: a reminder that “town and gown” conflict is nearly as old as the university itself.

📜 PRIMARY SOURCE
Royal Charter to the University of Cambridge, 1231 (excerpted and lightly adapted)
Trans. after C. H. Cooper, Annals of Cambridge, 1842 (public-domain-era scholarly translation); excerpt.
“The King to the Sheriff of Cambridge, greeting. We command you that you cause it to be publicly proclaimed… that no one henceforth presume to receive any scholar in our town of Cambridge unless he be under the discipline of some Master… And we forbid that any of the said scholars be aggrieved in his person or goods contrary to the custom of the University, upon pain of forfeiture.”
📌 A NOTE FOR YOU

The king is here enforcing university discipline and protecting scholars from harm at the same time: recognition that this fragile new institution needed the crown's active protection to survive and grow in the middle of an ordinary market town.
